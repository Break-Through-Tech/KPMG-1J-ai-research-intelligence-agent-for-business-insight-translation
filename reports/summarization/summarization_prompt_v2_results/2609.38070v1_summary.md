**Research Objective**  
Investigate how to quantify the uncertainty of large language model (LLM) answers by estimating confidence in their chain‑of‑thought reasoning, and develop a simple yet effective signal for calibration. [2609.38070v1]

**Methodology**  
- Propose **Divergent Token Confidence (DTC)**, which counts tokens where two models strongly disagree during decoding. Disagreement is measured with the Jensen–Shannon divergence (JSD) between the next‑token distributions of the generator and an auxiliary model; tokens whose JSD exceeds a threshold θ are marked as divergent. [2609.38070v1]  
- Define two estimators: **DTClin** (maps the divergent‑token count directly to a confidence score) and **DTCprod** (combines the count with a full‑sequence probability score). [2609.38070v1]  
- Apply DTC in both **white‑box** settings (using the generator’s logits) and **black‑box** settings (using auxiliary models without access to logits). [2609.38070v1]  
- Conduct experiments on multiple model families and six mathematical benchmarks, comparing DTC against probability‑based, verbalized‑confidence, and UQAC baselines. Calibration is evaluated with Expected Calibration Error (ECE) using 20 confidence bins. [2609.38070v1]

**Key Findings**  
- The number of divergent tokens is **almost negatively associated** with path accuracy, indicating it is a useful uncertainty signal. [2609.38070v1]  
- In white‑box evaluation, the count‑only estimator (DTClin) achieves an **average ECE of 13.0 %**, markedly lower than the **32.7 %–42.4 %** range of standard full‑sequence confidence methods. [2609.38070v1]  
- In black‑box settings, DTC improves calibration on DeepSeek‑V3.2, reducing mean ECE from **32.1 %–40.2 %** to **13.7 %–16.3 %**. [2609.38070v1]  
- Across all tested model families and benchmarks, DTC provides better calibration than probability‑based, verbalized‑confidence, and UQAC baselines, and also mitigates overconfidence in verbalized scores. [2609.38070v1]
